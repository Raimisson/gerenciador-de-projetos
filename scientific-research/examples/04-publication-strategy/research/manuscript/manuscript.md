# Revenue decoupling and utility energy efficiency programs: a structured rapid review of the quantitative evidence

## Abstract

Revenue decoupling is widely presented as a regulatory tool that removes the throughput incentive of energy utilities, but the empirical literature on its effects on utility energy efficiency programs is small and difficult to access. We conducted a structured rapid review with a reproducible search log, forward citation chasing and explicit screening criteria. Of the records identified, none could be formally included because the full texts of the potentially eligible studies were not accessible; one study reports, at abstract level, an empirical association between decoupling and higher demand-side management spending. As contextual evidence, a descriptive study of United States utilities documents small average rate adjustments associated with decoupling mechanisms. No verifiable estimate of the magnitude of decoupling effects on efficiency programs was found in the accessible sources. We discuss implications for regulators considering decoupling, including in jurisdictions outside the United States, and outline the evidence needed to close this gap.

**Keywords:** revenue decoupling; energy efficiency programs; demand-side management; utility regulation; evidence review

## 1. Introduction

<!-- P-I1 -->
Utilities whose revenues depend on volumetric sales have weak incentives to promote energy efficiency, and revenue decoupling was designed to break this link. Whether decoupling changes utility behaviour towards efficiency programs is an empirical question with direct relevance for regulators.

<!-- P-I2 -->
This paper asks which studies have quantitatively measured the effects of revenue decoupling on utility energy efficiency programs, and what those studies allow regulators to conclude.

## 2. Methods

<!-- P-M1 -->
We searched the Scite index with two Boolean strings and complemented the search with forward citation chasing from a seed study; attempts in two other academic search services failed and are documented in the search log. The search returned 29849 and 806 hits for the two strings, of which the top-ranked 10 and 6 records were exported, and forward citation chasing added 9 records [E-0006]. Screening followed pre-specified inclusion and exclusion criteria, and all decisions are recorded with reason codes.

## 3. Results

<!-- P-R1 -->
Of 25 records identified, 3 were duplicates, 22 were screened and 17 were excluded at title or abstract level; the full texts of 4 potentially eligible studies could not be retrieved and the only full text assessed was excluded because its outcome was tariff impacts rather than efficiency programs, leaving no formally included study [E-0006].

<!-- P-R2 -->
According to its abstract, Kahn-Lang (2016) reports that decoupling is historically associated with larger demand-side management spending and higher investment efficacy [E-0001]. The abstract does not report magnitudes, and the empirical design could not be assessed without the full text.

<!-- P-R3 -->
As contextual evidence, Cappers et al. (2020) document, for 21 utilities in 11 US states and the District of Columbia between 2005 and 2017, average annual decoupling rate adjustments of 0.4 percent of retail rates, with a median of 0.2 percent [E-0002; E-0003]. This descriptive study does not estimate effects on efficiency programs.

## 4. Discussion

<!-- P-D1 -->
The accessible evidence does not allow an estimate of how much decoupling changes utility efficiency programs [E-0005]. Two studies identified through citation chasing are priority candidates for full-text review, and their content has not yet been verified.

<!-- P-D2 -->
Regulators outside the United States should treat the available evidence as context-specific: differences in tariff-setting regimes and in legal obligations to invest in energy efficiency may change how decoupling operates.

## 5. Limitations

<!-- P-L1 -->
The review relied on one bibliographic index plus citation chasing, exported only top-ranked records, and could not access several full texts; the absence of other studies in this search does not prove that they do not exist.

## 6. Conclusions

<!-- P-C1 -->
Decoupling is frequently discussed as an enabler of utility energy efficiency, but verifiable quantitative evidence on its effects on efficiency programs was not found in the accessible sources. Full-text review of the identified candidates is the most direct next step.
