Revenue decoupling is often presented as a way to remove utilities' throughput incentive, yet the quantitative evidence on its effects on utility energy efficiency programs is small and hard to access.

Using a reproducible search log, forward citation chasing and explicit screening criteria, we map what this evidence allows regulators to conclude. One study reports, at abstract level, an association between decoupling and higher demand-side management spending; contextual descriptive evidence documents average decoupling rate adjustments of 0.4 percent of retail rates in 21 US utilities between 2005 and 2017. No verifiable estimate of the magnitude of decoupling effects on efficiency programs was found in the accessible sources, and we identify the studies whose full-text review is the most direct next step.

We believe the manuscript is relevant to readers of Utilities Policy because it addresses a regulatory design question for energy utilities and engages with work published in the journal, notably von Loessl and Wetzel (2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jup.2022.101416).
